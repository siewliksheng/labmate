# Chemical Waste Disposal

Chemical waste (including formaldehyde, other fixatives, solvents, and
any reagent with a hazardous-chemical SDS classification) must never be
disposed of via the biohazard waste stream, autoclave waste, or regular
trash, regardless of the volume spilled or discarded. Biohazard bins are
for biological waste; they are not rated or permitted for chemical waste,
and mixing the two creates a disposal hazard for whoever handles the bin
downstream.

Spilled chemical waste, including material used to absorb or wipe up a
spill (paper towels, absorbent pads), is itself chemical waste and follows
the same disposal path as the original chemical -- check that specific
chemical's SDS disposal section for the correct waste stream (e.g.
formaldehyde-contaminated waste typically requires a labeled hazardous
chemical waste container, not general trash or biohazard disposal).

A spill being small does not change which waste stream applies. When in
doubt about the correct disposal path for a specific chemical, check its
SDS or contact the lab's chemical safety officer before disposing of
anything.
